Why publish here instead of letting code sit in a private folder?

Most student physics scripts stay half-finished on a hard drive. Grouping projects under Vectorial Collective gives small tools a clear home, basic code review, and visibility with other students working on similar math and simulation problems.

Shared GitHub workflows

Repos live under the collective on GitHub so contributors can open issues, send pull requests, and link repos to their member dashboard.

Focus on clear math and units

We care more about readable equations, explicit SI units, and edge-case handling than flashy wrappers.

Peer feedback in #the-forge

Bring half-baked ideas, C++ build questions, or numerical bugs to Discord before or after submitting a project.

Why this exists

I started Vectorial Collective in high school because I wanted a place to put physics and programming projects where other students building STEM tools could actually see them, test them, and give honest feedback.

Whether you are writing your first simulation script or polishing a solver, the goal is straightforward: give student STEM builders credit for their work and keep useful open-source tools accessible.
